Detailed metrics and methodology

Brier score measures probability error; lower is better. Alert thresholds target a 5% false-positive rate in earlier validation data. The rate on later data can be different. Catch rate counts detected events; false-positive rate counts alerts among periods without that event.

Models are selected monthly using outcomes already available then. Market conditions use trailing 24-hour returns and 30-day volatility. High volatility means at least 1.5 times the 30-day level. Only complete, settled, matching observations are scored. Historical receipt times are reconstructed, so these are not live results.

How to read a forecast

1. The end price

The midpoint is the model's central estimate. Lower, within range, and higher probabilities describe where the final price lands relative to the two stated move thresholds. These three probabilities add to 100%.

2. A move along the way

A large-rise probability means the price touches the upper threshold at any point during the window. The price can rise and then fall, so the two touch probabilities do not have to add to 100%.

3. The evidence

Less probability error does not mean the same percentage of predictions were correct. Check missed moves and false alarms too. An 80% price range is a model target; its actual hit rate is shown under Performance.

Observation starts at the next full UTC hour after the forecast's input slot. Every forecast shows its exact start and end. The live quote does not change an already published forecast. Model edges and live performance remain under evaluation.
